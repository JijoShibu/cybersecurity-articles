*Escaping the Resident Evil‑themed CTF by learning the basics of enumeration, cryptography, and steganography.*

Welcome to the Biohazard mansion! If you’re new to cybersecurity or Capture The Flag (CTF) challenges, this Resident Evil‑inspired room by @DesKel on [TryHackMe](https://medium.com/u/dc49a0a3cb16?source=post_page---user_mention--a0b2ef42f743------------------------------------------) is an incredible place to practice. Hitting my 100‑day learning streak on [TryHackMe](https://medium.com/u/dc49a0a3cb16?source=post_page---user_mention--a0b2ef42f743------------------------------------------) this month has been an awesome milestone, and it’s narrative‑driven labs exactly like this that make the ethical hacking journey so rewarding.

This room is less about hacking into highly secure, complex systems and more about solving puzzles, uncovering hidden clues, and cracking secret codes. Think of it like a digital escape room!

## File-analysis tools

- [**Steghide** ](https://steghide.com/)— hides/extracts data inside images.

- [**ExifTool** ](https://exiftool.org/)— reads metadata (comments, camera info, etc.).

- [**Binwalk**](https://www.kali.org/tools/binwalk/)— searches for embedded files.

## Conclusion

The Biohazard room is a masterclass in puzzle‑based CTFs. It forces you to look closely, learn different encodings, and follow a trail of logical clues. From enumeration and cryptography to steganography and privilege escalation, every skill I’d been practising on [TryHackMe](https://medium.com/u/dc49a0a3cb16?source=post_page---user_mention--a0b2ef42f743------------------------------------------) came together in one immersive story.

Huge thanks to @DesKel for the fantastic theme and plot. If you haven’t tried this room yet, I highly recommend it — and if you have, let me know in the comments which puzzle was your favourite!

Happy hacking! 🎮💻
